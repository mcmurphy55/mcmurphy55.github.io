Privacy Policy

Last updated November 22, 2026

Us has no account, no server of its own, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. The developer never receives your recordings, photos, stories or the people in your family tree.

What Us keeps, and where

Us keeps what you make in it: the people you add (their names, the pictures you choose, how they are related, and the details you enter about them, such as years of birth and death), the stories you record (recordings, notes, photos, the questions asked, and the moments you mark), their transcripts and summaries, About pages, the questions you save, and your settings.

  • On your iPhone. Everything is kept in Us on your iPhone.
  • In your private iCloud. While Keep in iCloud is on (it is on unless you turn it off), the same library is kept in the private iCloud of the Apple Account signed in on your iPhone, so it appears on your other iPhones. It is stored by Apple in your account; the developer cannot read it. You can turn it off in Us under Settings → iCloud.

Microphone, camera, photos and the local network

Us uses the microphone only to record, after you tap Start recording. Recording goes on if you lock the screen or leave Us, until you stop it; a phone call pauses it. The camera is used only when you take a photo for a story, and only the photos you choose from your library are added. The local network is used only while you hand an album over to an iPhone nearby, or receive one (see Sharing an album); iOS asks you to allow it the first time.

Transcripts, summaries and Apple Intelligence

  • Transcripts are made on your iPhone with Apple’s speech recognition.
  • While recording, Us may list the people and places mentioned so far. Apple Intelligence writes the list on your iPhone; the words it heard are forgotten when you stop, and nothing of it is saved.
  • Summaries are written on your iPhone by Apple Intelligence.
  • About pages, the check for misheard words, and summaries of stories too long for your iPhone use Apple’s Private Cloud Compute, only once you write an About page, tap Check for misheard words in a transcript, or turn Use Private Cloud Compute on. The text of the transcripts concerned, with the names in them, is then sent to Apple’s Private Cloud Compute. For an About page, the stories’ titles and dates and that person’s year of birth go with it and, to tell who a mentioned name is, the names of the people in your family tree, how they are related and their years of birth. For the check for misheard words, the names of the people in your family tree and how they are related go with it, and the names of people and places found in your other stories. Apple says data sent there is used only to fulfill the request and is neither stored nor made accessible to Apple. The developer never receives it. You can turn it off in Us under Settings → Apple Intelligence.
  • Translations are made on your iPhone with Apple’s Translation.

Sharing an album

Nothing is shared unless you hand an album over. You do that with the other person beside you: your iPhone shows a four-digit number, they enter it in Us on their iPhone, and a copy of the album you chose goes from your iPhone to theirs.

  • What goes. The saved stories told by the person whose album it is, with their recordings and photos, the transcripts and summaries, the line chosen to quote from each story, their About page, and the covers; their name, their picture, and the details you entered about them; and the names and pictures of you and of anyone who told a story with them. Photos go as the files you added: a photo chosen from your library can keep what is saved inside it, such as when and where it was taken. Your notes are never included. Drafts, your saved questions and your settings stay out too, and so do stories you kept from an album someone else handed to you. The rest of your family tree stays out as well: the other people in it, and how anyone is related. What a story itself says goes with it, though: a question that named a family member (“What was Joe like…”) and the names spoken in it. So does what the check for misheard words noted on a transcript: a note can give a name from your family tree, or a name or place from your other stories, as the likely spelling.
  • How it travels. Straight between the two iPhones over the local network, encrypted with a key made from the number. The number is short so that it can be typed: it keeps the album from reaching the wrong iPhone, and Us stops after ten wrong numbers, but it is not strong protection against someone nearby who sets out to intercept the album while the number is showing. It does not pass through the internet, iCloud or any server, and the developer never receives it. Show the number only to the person you mean to hand the album to.
  • What the two iPhones tell each other. Each shows the other the name its owner gave themselves in Us, if they gave one. Before the album arrives, the receiving iPhone is also told whose album it is, how many stories it holds, and its size.
  • What the other person can do. Listen and read. They cannot change your album. They can keep its stories in their own library; those copies are then theirs, kept on their iPhone and, while Keep in iCloud is on there, in their own private iCloud, and they can edit, export or delete them.
  • It cannot be taken back. An album you have handed over stays on the other iPhone until that person removes it. You cannot remove or change it from your iPhone, and what you add, change or delete later does not reach them. Handing the album over again replaces the copy they were given and adds its new stories; stories they have already kept in their own library stay as they were.

Exports

When you export an album, Us makes a ZIP file on your iPhone and you choose where it goes. If you include photos, they go in as the files you added: a photo chosen from your library can keep what is saved inside it, such as when and where it was taken. What happens to the ZIP then depends on where you send it.

Purchases

Unlock Us is sold by Apple through the App Store. Apple handles the payment; Us learns only whether the purchase is active on your Apple Account.

Recording other people

Everyone you record should know and agree. In some places the law requires the consent of everyone in a conversation before it is recorded. Us reminds you before you record, but getting that agreement is up to you.

Children

Us is meant to be used by adults. Families may record stories with their children; those stories stay in the family’s own library as described above, and the developer never receives them.

Deleting your data

  • Delete a story or a person in Us, and it is removed from your iPhone and from your iCloud.
  • Settings → Reset Us erases the library on your iPhone and, if you choose Erase here and in iCloud, in iCloud too.
  • Deleting the app removes what is on your iPhone. What is in iCloud can also be deleted in the Settings app, under your Apple Account’s iCloud storage.
  • An album you have handed over, and a ZIP you have exported, stay with whoever has them. Deleting in Us does not remove those copies.
  • An album handed to you can be removed in Us with Remove from this iPhone. Stories you kept from it stay in your library until you delete them.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it.
